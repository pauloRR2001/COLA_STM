Lynk Global Flight Dynamics Challenge - Requirements Summary
============================================================

Purpose
-------
Prepare a candidate response for a 2-hour interactive engineering panel discussion. The challenge evaluates:

1. First-principles orbital mechanics.
2. Practical operational trade studies.
3. Ability to build right-sized automation tools.
4. Ability to turn ambiguous operational problems into useful engineering requirements.
5. Judgment in constellation flight dynamics, collision avoidance, and anomaly recovery.

The expected effort is approximately 4-6 hours. The response may be a slide deck, technical write-up, spreadsheet, or other effective medium. Assumptions should be explicitly documented.


Scenario
--------
A LEO Walker constellation is being deployed. Satellites are injected directly into a lower circular operational drift orbit to optimize launch vehicle performance and remove altitude margins from the launch manifest.

Plane separation is achieved passively through J2-driven differential RAAN precession between the lower drift orbit and the final operational orbit.

The launch campaign occurs during Solar Maximum, so elevated and variable thermospheric density produces significant aerodynamic drag uncertainty.


Fixed Baseline Parameters
-------------------------
Spacecraft:
- Mass: 420 kg
- Propulsion: single 35 mN Hall-effect thruster
- Specific impulse: Isp = 1400 s
- Thruster used for stationkeeping and maneuvers

Orbits:
- Initial launch / drift orbit: 330 km circular, 51.6 deg inclination
- Target operational orbit: 520 km circular, 51.6 deg inclination

Drag configuration:
- Nominal ram-face drag area: A_ram = 0.65 m^2
- Maximum drag area: A_max = 21.5 m^2
- Tumbling drag area: A_tumble = 9.5 m^2
- Drag coefficient: Cd = 2.2

Environment:
- Solar Maximum conditions
- Expected decay rate at 330 km under nominal ram-face attitude: -0.512 km/day


Part 1 Requirements: Orbit Raising, Phasing, and Differential Drag
------------------------------------------------------------------
Given:
- Satellite remains in the drift orbit for exactly 110 days.
- Two strategies are compared.

Strategy A: Passive Decay
- No maneuvers during the drift phase.
- Satellite naturally decays from 330 km to approximately 273.7 km.
- Then performs a continuous low-thrust raise to the 520 km operational orbit.
- Drift-phase HET delta-V: 0 m/s.
- Orbit-raise delta-V to 520 km: approximately 140.4 m/s.

Strategy B: Maintained Altitude
- Periodic prograde HET top-up burns counteract drag.
- Satellite remains near 330 km throughout the 110-day drift.
- Then performs the final raise to 520 km.
- Drift-phase HET delta-V: approximately 68.5 m/s.
- Orbit-raise delta-V to 520 km: approximately 108.2 m/s.

Candidate should address:
1. Compute and compare total delta-V for Strategy A and Strategy B.
2. Explain why Strategy A can have lower total delta-V despite beginning the final raise from a lower altitude.
3. Explain how J2 differential RAAN drift changes as altitude decays.
4. Discuss how Strategy A's changing altitude affects RAAN targeting accuracy and constellation plane placement.
5. Discuss operational risks of allowing decay to about 273.7 km during Solar Maximum.
6. Compare propellant savings against lifetime, recovery, drag uncertainty, and collision-risk margins.
7. State which strategy is preferred, or define a decision rule for selecting between them.
8. Document all assumptions.

Important equations to include:
- J2 nodal precession:
  Omega_dot = -(3/2) * J2 * n * (R_E/p)^2 * cos(i)

- Circular mean motion:
  n = sqrt(mu/a^3)

- Low-thrust acceleration:
  a_T = T/m

- Mass depletion:
  mdot = -T/(Isp*g0)


Part 2 Requirements: Low-Thrust COLA and Differential-Drag Mitigation
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The candidate should design a minimum viable automated CDM-to-maneuver-recommendation pipeline.

Candidate should address:
1. Explain why low-thrust COLA differs from impulsive COLA.
2. Account for finite-burn arcs and earlier decision timelines.
3. Discuss how covariance evolves during continuous-thrust maneuvers.
4. Define how to evaluate whether a maneuver reduces risk sufficiently.
5. Discuss whether differential drag can mitigate conjunctions by changing satellite attitude.
6. Estimate along-track timing change from changing drag area.
7. Explain when differential drag is preferable to HET thrust and when it is not.
8. Propose a minimum viable automated pipeline for conjunction response.
9. Identify which parts should initially remain human-in-the-loop.
10. Identify validation, audit, and safety checks needed before automation can scale.

Minimum pipeline functions:
- Ingest CDM or conjunction alert.
- Validate object identifiers, epochs, states, frames, and covariance data.
- Normalize states and covariances to a common frame/time system.
- Propagate primary and secondary objects to TCA.
- Compute miss distance, covariance geometry, and probability of collision.
- Generate candidate mitigations:
  * low-thrust burn options,
  * differential-drag attitude options,
  * no-maneuver option.
- Score each option by collision-risk reduction, delta-V, propellant impact, coverage impact, schedule impact, and downstream conjunction risk.
- Recommend an action.
- Route recommendation for human approval.
- Generate command products after approval.
- Monitor post-action orbit and update the plan.

Important equations to include:
- Drag acceleration:
  a_D = -0.5 * rho * v_rel^2 * Cd * (A/m) * vhat_rel

- Ballistic coefficient:
  B = m/(Cd*A)

- Mean motion:
  n = sqrt(mu/a^3)

- Approximate mean-motion change:
  Delta_n/n ~= -(3/2) * Delta_a/a

- Along-track separation accumulation:
  Delta_s(t) ~= a * integral(Delta_n(t) dt)

- Timing shift at TCA:
  Delta_t_TCA ~= Delta_s_TCA/v


Part 3 Requirements: Operational Risk and Anomaly Management
------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario:
- Immediately after launch, the spacecraft loses attitude control and tumbles.
- Effective drag area increases from A_ram = 0.65 m^2 to A_tumble = 9.5 m^2.

Candidate should address:
1. Compute the drag-area multiplier:
   A_tumble/A_ram = 9.5/0.65 ~= 14.6

2. Estimate the new altitude decay rate at 330 km under first-order scaling:
   hdot_tumble ~= 14.6 * (-0.512 km/day) ~= -7.5 km/day

3. Define and justify a point-of-no-return altitude.
4. Estimate the number of days available before reaching the point of no return.
5. Discuss uncertainty in density, attitude state, drag coefficient, and thruster availability.
6. Define telemetry or orbit-determination triggers for declaring an emergency recovery mode.
7. Recommend operational thresholds and response timelines.
8. Explain how the recovery logic should be automated or decision-supported.

The point of no return is not explicitly specified in the challenge. The candidate must define it. A defensible definition is:
- the altitude at which maximum available thrust can no longer overcome high-percentile drag with sufficient recovery margin, or
- the altitude below which the remaining time before reentry is shorter than the combined ADCS recovery, orbit determination, maneuver planning, approval, and thrust-raise timeline.


Simulation Requirements for a Programmatic Solution
---------------------------------------------------
A challenge-faithful simulation should include:

1. State propagation:
   - Cartesian Cowell propagation or equivalent.
   - Earth two-body gravity.
   - J2 perturbation.
   - Atmospheric drag.
   - Continuous low thrust.
   - Mass depletion.

2. Drag model:
   - At minimum, calibrated to reproduce the provided decay rate of -0.512 km/day at 330 km with A_ram = 0.65 m^2 and Cd = 2.2.
   - Optionally, use an exponential density model calibrated to the same decay rate.
   - Optionally, use NRLMSISE-00, JB2008, DTM, or another atmosphere model, but the result should be benchmarked against the challenge-specified decay rate.

3. Attitude/area modes:
   - Nominal ram-face mode: A = 0.65 m^2.
   - Maximum drag mode: A = 21.5 m^2.
   - Tumbling mode: A = 9.5 m^2.

4. Thrust model:
   - T = 35 mN.
   - Isp = 1400 s.
   - Thrust direction should be documented.
   - For orbit raising and stationkeeping, a tangential/prograde steering law is acceptable for a first-order simulation.

5. Outputs:
   - altitude vs. time,
   - semi-major axis vs. time,
   - eccentricity vs. time,
   - inclination vs. time,
   - RAAN vs. time,
   - RAAN drift rate vs. time,
   - delta-V used,
   - propellant mass used,
   - time to reach target orbit,
   - time to reach recovery threshold,
   - along-track displacement for differential-drag cases,
   - COLA risk before and after mitigation.

6. Validation checks:
   - Strategy A should roughly reproduce 330 km to 273.7 km after 110 days.
   - Strategy A orbit raise delta-V should be compared against approximately 140.4 m/s.
   - Strategy B drift maintenance delta-V should be compared against approximately 68.5 m/s.
   - Strategy B orbit raise delta-V should be compared against approximately 108.2 m/s.
   - Tumbling decay should show order-of-magnitude agreement with the 14.6x area multiplier estimate.

7. Sensitivity analysis:
   - density multiplier,
   - drag coefficient uncertainty,
   - area uncertainty,
   - thrust duty cycle,
   - missed thrust events,
   - delayed ADCS commissioning,
   - recovery altitude threshold,
   - covariance/process noise for COLA.


Simulation Fidelity Guidance
----------------------------
Minimum acceptable model:
- Two-body + J2 + calibrated drag + low thrust + mass depletion.

Useful enhanced model:
- Exponential atmosphere calibrated to challenge data.
- Earth atmosphere co-rotation for relative drag velocity.
- J2 plus optional J3/J4.
- Event detection for altitude thresholds and maneuver transitions.
- Monte Carlo or grid sweep over density uncertainty.

Operational-grade model, not required by the challenge:
- High-degree gravity.
- NRLMSISE-00/JB2008 with solar and geomagnetic drivers.
- Earth orientation parameters, precession/nutation, polar motion.
- Third-body Sun/Moon gravity.
- Solar radiation pressure.
- Full covariance propagation and CDM covariance handling.
- Integrated scheduling, command generation, and post-maneuver orbit determination.

Precession, nutation, polar motion, and full EOP handling are not explicitly required by the challenge. They may be appropriate for an operational tool, but the challenge primarily tests J2, drag, low-thrust maneuvering, and operational decision logic.


Make-vs-Buy / Architecture Requirements
---------------------------------------
A strong candidate should not propose writing every astrodynamics component from scratch. The recommended architecture is layered:

Layer 1: Scientific kernel
- frames,
- time systems,
- Earth orientation,
- gravity models,
- atmosphere models,
- propagators,
- event detection,
- SPICE/ephemeris support.

These should generally be bought, adopted, or reused from validated tools such as Orekit, GMAT, CSPICE, Tudat, STK/ODTK, or equivalent.

Layer 2: Flight dynamics library
- maneuver design,
- low-thrust finite-burn modeling,
- stationkeeping,
- covariance propagation,
- COLA geometry,
- differential-drag utilities,
- constellation phasing utilities.

This may be a mix of adopted and custom code.

Layer 3: Operations decision engine
- CDM prioritization,
- maneuver recommendation logic,
- fleet impact scoring,
- coverage impact scoring,
- differential-drag vs thrust trade,
- anomaly recovery logic,
- decision thresholds,
- audit logging.

This should mostly be custom because it encodes company-specific operations policy.

Layer 4: Mission software / operator workflow
- dashboards,
- APIs,
- command generation,
- approval workflow,
- telemetry feedback,
- reporting,
- training and procedures.

This should mostly be custom.

Core principle:
- Buy or adopt validated physics infrastructure.
- Build the mission-specific decision logic that differentiates the company.


Reviewer Emphasis
-----------------
The most important aspects to evaluate are:

1. Does the candidate understand the orbital mechanics?
2. Does the candidate recognize drag uncertainty as a dominant risk under Solar Maximum?
3. Does the candidate trade propellant against lifetime, recovery margin, schedule, and collision risk?
4. Does the candidate understand low-thrust COLA as a finite-time decision problem?
5. Does the candidate propose automation that scales from a few satellites to hundreds?
6. Does the candidate keep safety-critical execution human-in-the-loop at first?
7. Does the candidate document assumptions and turn missing information into operational requirements?
8. Does the candidate distinguish between reusable astrodynamics infrastructure and company-specific operational intelligence?

The challenge is best interpreted as a scalable flight-dynamics operations design problem, not merely an orbit-mechanics calculation exercise.
